## 1. Same day, two growth stories: Unilever grows on volume, Danone on price
- **Source:** Unilever Q3 2026 trading statement, 28 Oct 2026 (https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/unilever--ulvr/q3-2026-trading-statement/9844580) · Danone Q3 2026 sales, 28 Oct 2026 (https://www.ideal-investisseur.fr/en/stock-news/danone-sales-up-4-in-third-quarter-driven-mainly-by-price-increases/28025.html)
- **Region / theme / pillar:** Europe / global · Brand love & RGM · P1, P2
- **Summary:** Unilever reported 5.2% underlying sales growth, with 2.9% from volume and 2.3% from price, and now
  expects at least 3% volume growth for 2026. Danone grew 4% like-for-like, driven mostly by price
  (about 3.6 pts price vs 0.4 pts volume/mix ⚠️ sources differ on the split).
- **Why your audience cares:** Every GCC GM is planning 2027 pricing right now. Asking "do we grow on price or
  on volume?" is the board-level question this news lets you answer.
- **Your angle:** Price-led growth spends brand equity, while volume-led growth shows you've earned it. Brand love
  is what lets you take price *and* keep volume.

**Draft post**

> Two of Europe's biggest FMCG companies reported Q3 on the same day. The headline numbers looked similar. The stories underneath were opposite.
>
> Unilever: 5.2% growth, more than half of it from volume.
> Danone: 4% growth, almost all of it from price.
>
> Neither is "wrong". But after 15+ years in FMCG across Europe, Türkiye and the Gulf, I've learned to read these two kinds of growth very differently.
>
> Price-led growth spends brand equity. It works until the shopper decides you're no longer worth it.
> Volume-led growth shows you've earned the right to the price you charge.
>
> In a 70% inflation market I learned that the brands that kept volume while taking price were never the cheapest. They were the most loved. People made room in a shrinking basket for them.
>
> That's the real job of brand love: it's not a "soft" metric, it's what gives your price list permission.
>
> As you build your 2027 plans: how much of your growth will come from price, and how much from people choosing you more often?
>
> #FMCG #RevenueGrowthManagement #BrandBuilding #Marketing #GCC

*First comment:* Sources: Unilever Q3 2026 trading statement and Danone Q3 2026 sales release (links above).

## 2. Premium in Western Europe now needs "proof, not positioning"
- **Source:** NielsenIQ, "Premium has a new price in Western Europe FMCG: Proof, not positioning", 2026 (https://nielseniq.com/global/en/insights/analysis/2026/premium-has-a-new-price-in-west-europe-fmcg/) ⚠️ published before this week. Confirm the date.
- **Region / theme / pillar:** Europe · Premiumization · P3
- **Summary:** NIQ says Premium+ is 28.2% of Western European FMCG value and growing 4.1% year on year. Consumers are
  trading up and down at the same time, and a high price alone no longer signals value. Proof does.
- **Why your audience cares:** GCC premium is also growing. The European lesson ("prove it, don't price it") applies directly.
- **Your angle:** You doubled absolute revenue through premiumization, and it worked because it started from shopper
  and consumer insight, not from a higher price point.

**Draft post**

> "Premium" used to mean a higher price and a nicer pack. Not anymore.
>
> New NielsenIQ data from Western Europe: premium tiers are 28.2% of FMCG value and still growing, even as shoppers cut back elsewhere. The catch: price is no longer the signal. Proof is.
>
> I've led a premiumization strategy that doubled absolute revenue. What made it work wasn't the price ladder. It was three things:
>
> 1️⃣ Starting from the occasion: when and why does someone want to treat themselves?
> 2️⃣ Giving the shopper a reason they can see at shelf, not just in the ad.
> 3️⃣ Getting trade partners to believe in it as much as consumers did, because premium dies in the wrong channel.
>
> Premium isn't a price point. It's a promise you can show.
>
> For those of you running premium portfolios in the GCC: what's the "proof" your shoppers respond to most?
>
> #Premiumization #FMCG #ConsumerInsights #BrandStrategy #Marketing

## 3. UAE & Saudi shoppers are trading up *and* down, and the shelf is getting crowded
- **Source:** Communicate Online, "Middle East consumer spending rises as shoppers seek value across channels: report" (NielsenIQ data) (https://communicateonline.me/news/middle-east-consumer-spending-rises-as-shoppers-seek-value-across-channels-report/) ⚠️ confirm the publication date and figures.
- **Region / theme / pillar:** Middle East (UAE, KSA) · Premiumization / RGM · P2, P3
- **Summary:** The combined UAE + KSA consumer basket is reported at $56.2B. Premium FMCG grew 19% (UAE) and 14% (KSA),
  while value grew 17% and 19%. Active FMCG brands rose 6% in the UAE (13,000+) and 8% in KSA (11,000+).
- **Why your audience cares:** This is home turf for your readers. A polarised shopper plus more competing brands means the
  mid-tier is under pressure.
- **Your angle:** When both ends grow, the answer isn't to pick one. It's price-pack architecture that serves both
  occasions without cannibalising the core, which is what you did when reshaping the UAE go-to-market.

**Draft post**

> The Gulf shopper is doing two things at once.
>
> In the UAE, premium FMCG grew 19%. Value grew 17%.
> In Saudi Arabia, premium grew 14%. Value grew 19%.
>
> Meanwhile, the UAE now has 13,000+ active FMCG brands and Saudi 11,000+.
>
> More brands, a split shopper, and a squeezed middle. If your portfolio lives in the middle tier, 2027 is the year to act.
>
> When I reshaped a portfolio's go-to-market in the UAE, the breakthrough came from mapping every decision to an *occasion*: which brand, which pack, at which price, in which channel. That map showed us where to trade people up and where to protect entry price points, without the two fighting each other.
>
> Result: the highest volume and revenue in a decade.
>
> Polarisation isn't a threat if your price-pack architecture is designed for it.
>
> GCC GMs and CMOs: are you seeing your mid-tier get squeezed too?
>
> #FMCG #UAE #SaudiArabia #RevenueGrowthManagement #PricePackArchitecture

## 4. Almarai: revenue up 11%, profit up 1%

- **Region / theme / pillar:** Middle East (KSA) · RGM · P2, P5
- **Summary:** The Gulf's largest food and beverage company grew Q3 revenue 11% to SAR 6.19bn, but net profit rose
  only 1% to SAR 617.8m because feed, shipping and energy-driven distribution costs rose.
- **Why your audience cares:** It's a regional bellwether, and "top line up, bottom line flat" is the margin squeeze
  every GCC FMCG board is discussing.
- **Your angle:** Revenue growth isn't the same as Revenue Growth *Management*. RGM is about the quality of growth:
  mix, pack and channel, not just more sales.
- **Note:** this is a peer company in the region. Keep the tone respectful and use it as a market lesson, not a critique.

**Draft post**

> Revenue +11%. Net profit +1%.
>
> Almarai's Q3 is a strong top-line story, and a reminder of what many GCC FMCG businesses are facing: costs (feed, shipping, energy, distribution) are eating growth before it reaches the bottom line.
>
> This is exactly why I believe Revenue Growth *Management* is different from revenue growth.
>
> When I built an RGM function from scratch across four GCC markets, the first question we asked wasn't "how do we sell more?" It was "which growth is worth having?"
>
> → Which packs carry margin, and which only carry volume?
> → Which channels cost more to serve than they return?
> → Where does the brand give us permission to price, and where doesn't it?
>
> Revenue growth tells you the brand is wanted. RGM makes sure that love actually pays.
>
> Finance and commercial leaders: is margin mix on your 2027 agenda, or still treated as a finance-only topic?
>
> #RevenueGrowthManagement #FMCG #SaudiArabia #GCC #CommercialStrategy

## 5. Türkiye's inflation is at its lowest since 2021. What should brands do when it cools?
- **Source:** Trading Economics, Sept 2026 CPI (released early Oct 2026) (https://tradingeconomics.com/turkey/inflation-cpi/news/589435)
- **Region / theme / pillar:** Türkiye · Marketing through inflation · P4
- **Summary:** Türkiye's annual inflation slowed to 29.73% in September, from 31.51% in August. That was below the
  expected 30.3% and the lowest since November 2021. October CPI is due on 3 Nov. Consider posting right after that release.
- **Why your audience cares:** Many GCC GMs oversee Türkiye or similar volatile markets, and recruiters value leaders who have
  managed both the way down and the way back up.
- **Your angle:** Everyone writes about marketing *in* a crisis. Few write about what to do as it ends: when shoppers
  stop stockpiling, price elasticity changes, and brands that cut investment find they have to buy back equity.

**Draft post**

> Türkiye's inflation just hit its lowest level since 2021.
>
> I led marketing there when inflation was around 70%. We achieved the company's highest-ever market share in that period while growing volume, revenue and brand equity. But here's what I didn't expect: the way *out* of high inflation is just as tricky as the way in.
>
> Three things change when inflation cools:
>
> 1️⃣ Shoppers stop panic-buying and start comparing again. Your price gaps suddenly matter more.
> 2️⃣ Private label, which gained trust during the crisis, doesn't simply go away.
> 3️⃣ Brands that cut investment to protect margin find their equity has quietly eroded.
>
> The brands that win the recovery are the ones that kept investing in being loved while everyone else was only managing price.
>
> Have you led a brand through a crisis *and* the recovery? Which phase was harder?
>
> #Türkiye #FMCG #MarketingStrategy #BrandEquity #Inflation
